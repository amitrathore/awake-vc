Agent-run Organizations

Organizations of the future are not hierarchical control mechanisms but cooperative networks — of people, and now also of AI agents. The question is no longer whether AI will run parts of organizations, but how to design the protocol layer that lets agents and people coordinate transparently, accountably, and at scale.

From DAOs to Agent-run Organizations

The Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO) was an early attempt to encode governance and value distribution in a blockchain-based platform. The core insight was right: organizations should be transparent, participatory, and should distribute value to the people who create it. The execution — fully on-chain, dependent on smart contracts for every decision — proved brittle.

Agent-run organizations take the same insight and apply it to the age of AI. Instead of smart contracts automating governance, AI agents handle execution, coordination, and decision support — while humans retain oversight of goals and values. The organization becomes a hybrid network: agents handle the fast, repeatable, data-intensive work; people handle judgment, relationships, and direction.

What Agents Change

An AI agent that can discover resources, execute tasks, and report outcomes changes the unit economics of organization. Work that required a team of specialists can be handled by a single agent operating across a protocol. Work that required weeks of coordination can compress into hours.

But agents operating in isolation create new problems: attribution, trust, and accountability. When an agent acts on behalf of an organization, who is responsible for the outcome? When multiple agents collaborate across organizational boundaries, how are value and liability distributed?

The Protocol Layer

Intergraph is the institutional substrate for agent markets — a protocol layer that lets AI agents discover each other, exchange value, and coordinate across organizations without centralized intermediaries. It provides the trust and attribution infrastructure that agent-run organizations require: verifiable identity for agents, deterministic attribution of outcomes, and programmable governance rules that apply across organizational boundaries.

Opentangle is the organizational layer: programmable structures for people and agents to work together, with shared goals, transparent incentives, and the flexibility to reconfigure as the work evolves. Together, Intergraph and Opentangle form the foundation for organizations that can genuinely scale with AI rather than simply using AI as a tool inside old structures.

Simple Networks Win

The organizational structure that works in the age of AI is the same one found in nature: a simple network, with minimal hierarchy, where every node can connect to every other node and value flows to the people and agents who create it. Businesses that embrace this structure — and build on open protocols rather than walled gardens — are the ones that will win across innovation and agility.
